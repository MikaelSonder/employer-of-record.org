California combines U.S. federal employment obligations with statewide wage, leave, payroll-tax, worker-classification, and separation-pay rules. Local ordinances can impose higher wage floors and additional requirements, so the work location must be validated before onboarding.

Scope note. This page is an operational compliance checklist, not legal or tax advice. Rates and thresholds must be revalidated for the payroll year, employer account, employee work location, and any industry-specific rule.

At-a-glance

Statewide wage floor

USD 16.90 per hour is California’s statewide minimum wage from January 1, 2026. A city, county, or industry rule may require a higher rate.

Paid sick leave

Covered employees generally accrue at least one hour per 30 hours worked, subject to California accrual, use, carryover, and front-load rules.

State disability insurance

SDI withholding: 1.3% of employee wages, with no annual wage ceiling stated for the 2026 operational baseline.

New-employer UI profile

UI/SUI: 3.4% and ETT: 0.1% on the first USD 7,000 in wages per employee per calendar year, subject to EDD account treatment.

Employment standards

Pay frequency and statements

Pay nonexempt employees no less frequently than semi-monthly, subject to role-specific rules. Provide an itemized wage statement with the information required by California Labor Code section 226, including gross wages, deductions, hours for qualifying employees, rates, pay period, employer identity, and employee identifiers.

Hours, overtime, and breaks

Assess California daily and weekly overtime, meal-period, rest-break, and recordkeeping rules. Do not rely solely on federal overtime concepts; California rules are often more protective and exemptions are fact-specific.

Workers’ compensation

Secure California workers’ compensation coverage for employees. Confirm coverage before the employee starts work and maintain the required notices and claim-handling process.

Leave programs

Coordinate paid sick leave, California State Disability Insurance, Paid Family Leave, protected leave, and any local or employer-plan benefits. PFL is a wage-replacement program; job protection may arise from separate laws.

Onboarding controls

  • Confirm the employee’s actual California work location, including remote-work address, because local minimum wage and ordinance coverage can differ by city or county.
  • Complete federal Form I-9 within the required federal timing and apply nondiscrimination safeguards in the work-authorization process.
  • Obtain federal Form W-4 and California Form DE 4, then set up federal and California tax withholding.
  • Issue the California wage-theft prevention notice when required and provide required workplace notices in the appropriate language and format.
  • Enroll the employee in payroll, paid-sick-leave tracking, workers’ compensation coverage, and applicable benefit and leave programs.
  • Document job duties, exempt/nonexempt status, pay basis, work schedule, supervisor, worksite, and reimbursement arrangements before the start date.
  • Run a local-law check for the employee’s city and county, including minimum wage, paid leave, scheduling, fair-chance, and other local employment ordinances.

Classification and separation

Employee versus contractor

California generally applies the ABC test for many worker-classification questions. A worker is presumed to be an employee unless the hiring entity can establish all applicable elements of the test or a statutory exception. Escalate every contractor engagement for jurisdiction-specific review.

Final pay

For a discharge, final wages are generally due at the time of termination. For a voluntary resignation with at least 72 hours’ notice, final wages are generally due on the final day; without that notice, they are generally due within 72 hours. Accrued, unused vacation is ordinarily treated as wages and must be addressed at separation.

Local-law review

California’s statewide requirements are a floor, not a complete local compliance answer. Before quoting pay or issuing an offer, check whether the worksite is covered by a municipal or county minimum wage, local paid-sick-leave ordinance, industry wage order, or other local employment standard. Use the higher applicable employee-protective standard where overlapping rules apply.
