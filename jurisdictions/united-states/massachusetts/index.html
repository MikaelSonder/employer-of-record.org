Executive Summary

An Employer of Record in Massachusetts is a structured arrangement in which a licensed entity becomes the formal legal employer of a worker who performs services for a client business, while the client retains day-to-day direction of the work itself. The function exists because engaging staff directly in Massachusetts normally requires a registered employer, a payroll infrastructure and ongoing compliance with Massachusetts employment, tax, insurance and labor rules.

Operationally, the Employer of Record issues the Massachusetts employment agreement, establishes state income tax withholding through MassTaxConnect, registers with the Department of Unemployment Assistance for unemployment insurance when liability arises, administers Paid Family and Medical Leave contributions and maintains payroll records and wage-payment compliance under the Massachusetts Wage Act.

The Massachusetts legal framework for this function is anchored in the Massachusetts Wage Act, the Massachusetts Minimum Fair Wage Law, the Massachusetts Paid Family and Medical Leave Law, the unemployment insurance framework and the Massachusetts anti-discrimination statute. Massachusetts has a statewide minimum wage of $15.00 per hour and particularly strict final-pay rules: employees discharged from employment must receive all earned wages on the day of discharge.

Cross-border relevance is substantial because many Employer of Record clients are companies based outside Massachusetts or outside the United States. These businesses rely on the Employer of Record to lawfully employ staff in Massachusetts, coordinate multi-state and federal payroll obligations, and administer Massachusetts-specific leave, unemployment insurance and wage-payment requirements.

Purpose

The purpose of the Employer of Record function is to allow a business to lawfully engage workers in Massachusetts without first establishing its own Massachusetts entity, while ensuring that payroll, tax withholding, unemployment insurance, paid family and medical leave and labor law obligations are met correctly from the outset.

It exists to convert a hiring intention into a compliant Massachusetts employment relationship, reducing the administrative burden and misclassification risk that would otherwise fall on an out-of-state or foreign business unfamiliar with Massachusetts payroll and labor law.

Primary Outcome

A compliant Massachusetts employment relationship in which the worker holds a valid employment agreement, payroll and statutory contributions are administered correctly, minimum wage, paid family and medical leave and Wage Act obligations are respected, and the client business retains operational direction of the work without carrying local employer-of-record liability.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Employer of Record work usually progresses from onboarding intent to ongoing payroll administration and eventual offboarding. It matters because Employer of Record work is a continuous operating relationship, not a single filing event.

1. Client and Role AssessmentConfirm the client's hiring intent, the role, reporting line and whether the arrangement will genuinely function as an employment relationship in Massachusetts.
2. Minimum Wage and Terms MappingConfirm the applicable Massachusetts minimum wage, tipped or non-tipped status, overtime status and benefit terms.
3. Agreement IssuanceIssue a Massachusetts employment agreement in the Employer of Record's name, specifying role, compensation, pay frequency and working terms in accordance with Massachusetts law.
4. DOR, DUA and PFML SetupEstablish withholding through MassTaxConnect, register for unemployment insurance with DUA when liability applies and configure PFML contributions or an approved private plan.
5. Payroll ExecutionCalculate gross pay, withhold Massachusetts and federal tax, administer PFML deductions and employer contributions, calculate unemployment insurance contributions and issue payroll documentation.
6. Ongoing Compliance AdministrationAdminister paid leave, PFML, unemployment insurance reporting, benefits and payroll recordkeeping as the employment relationship evolves.
7. Offboarding or TransitionProcess termination in line with the Massachusetts Wage Act, including same-day final payment for an employee discharged from employment, or support transfer to the client's own Massachusetts entity.
Typical OutputsSigned employment agreements, MassTaxConnect and DUA registration confirmations, payroll records, PFML filings, unemployment insurance reports, statutory remittances and termination documentation.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Massachusetts Have a Statutory Minimum Wage That an Employer of Record Must Apply?Yes. Massachusetts has a statewide minimum wage of $15.00 per hour. Tipped employees may be paid a lower cash wage only where applicable tip-credit requirements are satisfied.
Who Administers Payroll Tax and Unemployment Insurance for an Employer of Record in Massachusetts?The Massachusetts Department of Revenue administers state income tax withholding through MassTaxConnect. The Department of Unemployment Assistance administers unemployment insurance registration, contribution rates and quarterly reporting. The Department of Family and Medical Leave administers Paid Family and Medical Leave.
Is Massachusetts Employer Registration Mandatory for an Employer of Record Operating in Massachusetts?Yes. Employers must establish a withholding tax account with the Department of Revenue through MassTaxConnect and register an unemployment insurance account with the Department of Unemployment Assistance when statutory liability applies.
Can a Foreign or Out-of-State Company Use an Employer of Record Instead of Establishing a Massachusetts Entity?Yes. Many out-of-state and foreign companies use an Employer of Record to hire staff in Massachusetts lawfully without first establishing a local entity, while still meeting Massachusetts payroll, withholding, unemployment insurance, paid leave and labor law requirements.
Is Payroll Filing Alone Enough for Compliance?No. Correct Employer of Record compliance also requires worker classification, minimum wage alignment, paid family and medical leave administration, Wage Act compliance and, where relevant, multi-state or cross-border payroll coordination.
