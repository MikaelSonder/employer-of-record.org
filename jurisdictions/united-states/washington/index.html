Executive Summary

An Employer of Record in Washington is a structured arrangement in which a licensed entity becomes the formal legal employer of a worker who performs services for a client business, while the client retains day-to-day direction of the work itself. The function exists because engaging staff directly in Washington normally requires a registered employer, a payroll infrastructure and ongoing compliance with Washington employment, insurance and labor rules.

Operationally, the Employer of Record establishes Washington business licensing and employer registration, calculates payroll, administers unemployment insurance contributions, Paid Family and Medical Leave premiums, WA Cares Fund payroll deductions and workers' compensation coverage, and files quarterly tax-and-wage reports through the relevant state systems.

The Washington legal framework for this function is anchored in the Minimum Wage Act, Wage Rebate Act, Paid Family and Medical Leave program, WA Cares Fund and workers' compensation framework. Washington's statewide minimum wage is $17.13 per hour in 2026, with higher local rates potentially applying in cities such as Seattle and Tukwila. Overtime-exempt employees must generally receive at least 2.25 times the statewide minimum wage in 2026.

Cross-border relevance is substantial because many Employer of Record clients are companies based outside Washington or outside the United States. These businesses rely on the Employer of Record to lawfully employ staff in Washington, coordinate multi-state and federal payroll obligations, and administer Washington-specific paid leave, long-term care and workers' compensation requirements.

Purpose

The purpose of the Employer of Record function is to allow a business to lawfully engage workers in Washington without first establishing its own Washington entity, while ensuring that payroll, unemployment insurance, paid leave, WA Cares, workers' compensation and labor law obligations are met correctly from the outset.

It exists to convert a hiring intention into a compliant Washington employment relationship, reducing the administrative burden and misclassification risk that would otherwise fall on an out-of-state or foreign business unfamiliar with Washington payroll and labor law.

Primary Outcome

A compliant Washington employment relationship in which the worker holds a valid employment agreement, payroll and statutory contributions are administered correctly, state and local minimum wage, paid leave, WA Cares and labor standards obligations are respected, and the client business retains operational direction of the work without carrying local employer-of-record liability.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Employer of Record work usually progresses from onboarding intent to ongoing payroll administration and eventual offboarding. It matters because Employer of Record work is a continuous operating relationship, not a single filing event.

1. Client and Role AssessmentConfirm the client's hiring intent, the role, reporting line and whether the arrangement will genuinely function as an employment relationship in Washington.
2. Minimum Wage and Terms MappingConfirm the statewide or applicable local minimum wage, overtime status, exempt salary threshold and benefit terms according to the employee's work location.
3. Agreement IssuanceIssue a Washington employment agreement in the Employer of Record's name, specifying role, compensation, pay frequency and working terms in accordance with Washington law.
4. Business License and Account SetupComplete or update the Business License Application, obtain or confirm the Unified Business Identifier and establish required ESD, L&I, Paid Leave and WA Cares payroll administration.
5. Payroll ExecutionCalculate gross pay, calculate unemployment insurance and workers' compensation obligations, withhold Paid Family and Medical Leave and WA Cares premiums where applicable and issue payroll documentation.
6. Quarterly ReportingFile unemployment insurance tax-and-wage reports through ESD's Employer Account Management System and complete Paid Leave and WA Cares reporting and remittances through the relevant state accounts.
7. Offboarding or TransitionProcess termination in line with Washington wage-payment rules, including final wages at the end of the established pay period, or support transfer to the client's own Washington entity where one is later established.
Typical OutputsSigned employment agreements, business license and UBI records, ESD and L&I account confirmations, payroll records, quarterly tax-and-wage reports, Paid Leave and WA Cares remittances and termination documentation.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Washington Have a Statutory Minimum Wage That an Employer of Record Must Apply?Yes. Washington's 2026 statewide minimum wage is $17.13 per hour. Higher local minimum wage rates may apply, including in cities such as Seattle and Tukwila, depending on the employee's work location and the employer's size or revenue.
Who Administers Payroll Tax and Statutory Contributions for an Employer of Record in Washington?The Employment Security Department administers unemployment insurance and Paid Family and Medical Leave. The Department of Labor and Industries administers workers' compensation. The Washington Department of Revenue manages business licensing and employer registration, while the WA Cares Fund is administered through the Employment Security Department.
Is Washington Employer Registration Mandatory for an Employer of Record Operating in Washington?Yes. A Business License Application filed with the Washington Department of Revenue is used to establish the employer's state registration and can trigger unemployment insurance registration with the Employment Security Department and workers' compensation registration with Labor and Industries.
Can a Foreign or Out-of-State Company Use an Employer of Record Instead of Establishing a Washington Entity?Yes. Many out-of-state and foreign companies use an Employer of Record to hire staff in Washington lawfully without first establishing a local entity, while still meeting Washington payroll, paid leave, WA Cares, unemployment insurance and labor law requirements.
Is Payroll Filing Alone Enough for Compliance?No. Correct Employer of Record compliance also requires worker classification, state and local minimum wage alignment, Paid Family and Medical Leave, WA Cares, workers' compensation and, where relevant, multi-state or cross-border payroll coordination.
