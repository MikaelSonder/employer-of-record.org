Texas applies U.S. federal employment rules alongside a comparatively limited state payroll layer. There is no Texas individual income tax, but employers must manage Texas unemployment tax, workers’ compensation decisions, state payday requirements, new-hire reporting, required posters, and federal payroll and work-authorization obligations.

Scope note. This page is an operational compliance checklist, not legal or tax advice. Confirm payroll-year rates, TWC account status, worksite-specific rules, federal coverage, exemption status, contract terms, and industry requirements before onboarding or changing payroll configuration.

At-a-glance

Minimum wage

USD 7.25 per hour is the Texas minimum wage baseline, aligned with the federal FLSA rate. A covered federal contractor or another applicable rule may impose a higher floor.

State income tax

No Texas personal income tax. Texas employers do not withhold state individual income tax from employee wages; federal withholding still applies.

2026 new-employer UI

2.70% is the 2026 TWC entry-level rate for all industry groups. Texas law otherwise uses the NAICS industry average or 2.7%, whichever is higher.

UI taxable wage base

Texas unemployment tax applies to the first USD 9,000 in wages paid to each employee during the calendar year.

Employment standards

Pay frequency

Employees exempt from federal overtime provisions must be paid at least monthly. Other employees must be paid at least twice monthly. Post designated regular paydays conspicuously; if no payday is designated, Texas defaults to the first and fifteenth of each month.

Overtime and records

Texas generally follows federal FLSA minimum-wage and overtime standards. Covered nonexempt employees are generally owed 1.5 times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Maintain accurate time and payroll records, and validate all claimed exemptions.

Breaks and leave

Texas law does not generally require meal or rest breaks, holiday premium pay, or paid vacation. However, short rest breaks may be compensable under federal law, and written employer policies or agreements must be followed. Local paid-sick-leave ordinances require legal review because of litigation and preemption developments.

Payday Law controls

Pay employees in full, on time, and on scheduled paydays. Obtain valid written authorization before deductions unless the deduction is legally required or court ordered. Follow written policies for commissions, bonuses, vacation, sick time, severance, and other benefits.

Onboarding controls

  • Confirm the employee’s Texas work address, remote-work arrangement, work schedule, and any federal-contractor, public-sector, healthcare, construction, or industry-specific coverage.
  • Complete federal Form I-9 within the required federal timing and administer the work-authorization process without prohibited discrimination.
  • Collect federal Form W-4 and set up federal income-tax, FICA, Medicare, FUTA, and year-end Form W-2 processes; do not set up Texas state income-tax withholding.
  • Register for Texas UI when liable, set the TWC tax configuration, file quarterly wage reports electronically, and pay Texas UI taxes electronically.
  • Report new hires and rehires to the Texas Office of the Attorney General program within the applicable deadline.
  • Decide and document workers’ compensation coverage status with legal and insurance review; provide all required notices for the chosen approach.
  • Provide and post required federal and Texas workplace notices, including the Texas Payday Law and unemployment/payday posters where required.
  • Document job duties, exempt/nonexempt classification, compensation plan, pay frequency, worksite, expense-reimbursement approach, and any written benefit policies before the start date.

Classification and separation

Employee versus contractor

For Texas unemployment purposes, a worker is generally an employee when services are performed for wages under a contract for hire and the employer has the right of direction or control, even if that right is not exercised. Classification can differ by law, including federal wage-and-hour and tax rules. Escalate every contractor engagement for fact-specific review.

Final pay

For a discharge, layoff, or firing, pay final wages within six calendar days of the last day. For a resignation, retirement, or quit, pay final wages on the next regularly scheduled payday. Pay unused vacation or other benefits only when the written policy or agreement requires payment.

Local-law review

Texas local-law requirements can be limited or affected by state preemption, but employers should still check the employee’s municipality and county before onboarding. Review local ordinances, client-site rules, public-contract requirements, and any federal-contractor wage obligations. When an enforceable local, contractual, or federal requirement provides a higher protection, configure payroll and policy to that higher standard.
