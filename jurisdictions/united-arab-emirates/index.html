Core Function
Acting as the legal employer of a worker performing services in the United Arab Emirates on behalf of a client business, including employment contracts, work permits and residence visas, Wage Protection System payroll, UAE national pension administration where applicable, end-of-service benefits and UAE labour law compliance.
Primary Interfaces

Cross-Border Note
UAE Employer of Record arrangements commonly involve expatriate employees, immigration sponsorship, free-zone versus mainland jurisdiction selection, tax residence, GCC pension coordination and cross-border mobility across the Gulf region.
Executive Summary

An Employer of Record in the United Arab Emirates is a structured arrangement in which a local entity becomes the formal legal employer of a worker who performs services for a client business, while the client retains day-to-day direction of the work itself. The function exists because engaging staff in the UAE normally requires a compliant local employment relationship, payroll infrastructure, work permit and residence visa sponsorship for expatriates and ongoing compliance with UAE labour and immigration rules.

Operationally, the Employer of Record issues the UAE employment contract, obtains the applicable work permit and residence visa for foreign employees, administers payroll through the Wage Protection System where applicable, manages UAE national pension registration and contributions where applicable and administers statutory end-of-service benefits for expatriate employees.

The UAE legal framework for this function is anchored in Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 on the Regulation of Employment Relationships, its implementing regulations, the Employment of Foreign Nationals framework and emirate or free-zone specific rules where applicable. The UAE has no single universal statutory minimum wage for the general private-sector workforce; salary terms are principally governed by the employment contract, WPS rules and any applicable Emiratisation or work permit conditions.

Cross-border relevance is substantial because the UAE workforce is predominantly expatriate. Employer of Record arrangements may require coordination of mainland versus free-zone rules, work permits, residence visas, tax residence, GCC social security arrangements and regional mobility across the Gulf.

Purpose

The purpose of the Employer of Record function is to allow a business to lawfully engage workers in the United Arab Emirates without first establishing its own UAE legal entity, while ensuring that employment contracts, payroll, WPS, work permits, residence visas and labour law obligations are met correctly from the outset.

It exists to convert a hiring intention into a compliant UAE employment relationship, reducing the administrative burden and immigration and misclassification risk that would otherwise fall on a foreign business unfamiliar with UAE payroll, employment and visa law.

Primary Outcome

A compliant UAE employment relationship in which the worker holds a valid local employment contract and, where required, work permit and residence visa; payroll and WPS obligations are administered correctly; pension or end-of-service benefit obligations are respected; and the client business retains operational direction of the work without carrying local employer-of-record liability.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how Employer of Record services operate in the United Arab Emirates. The section matters because UAE employment practice combines a predominantly expatriate workforce, employer-sponsored immigration, WPS salary payment rules, mainland and free-zone jurisdictional distinctions and different pension or end-of-service regimes based on employee nationality and employment location.

Operational CultureUAE employment practice is formal, sponsor-led and document-oriented. Employment contracts, work permits, residence visas, WPS payroll, medical insurance and end-of-service benefit calculations are central to compliant employer operations.
Legal Framework OrientationThe federal UAE Labour Law applies broadly to mainland private-sector employment, while specific free zones may operate distinct employment regulations. Work permit and immigration status are foundational to expatriate employment.
Commercial ContextPayroll cost planning differs by worker category. Expatriate employees generally do not trigger employer social security contributions but require visa, medical insurance and end-of-service benefit planning. UAE national employees may require pension contributions, Emiratisation compliance and relevant authority registration.
Language ExpectationArabic is the official language. English is widely used in international business and employment administration; bilingual Arabic-English documentation may be appropriate or required depending on the employment framework and authority.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Employer of Record work usually progresses from onboarding intent to ongoing payroll administration and eventual offboarding. It matters because Employer of Record work is a continuous employment, payroll and immigration relationship, not a single filing event.

1. Client, Role and Jurisdiction AssessmentConfirm the client's hiring intent, the role, reporting line, employee nationality and whether a mainland or particular free-zone employment framework applies.
2. Terms, Visa and Pension MappingConfirm contractual salary, working time, leave, work permit category, residence visa route, WPS applicability and UAE national pension or expatriate end-of-service benefit treatment.
3. Contract IssuanceIssue the UAE employment contract in the Employer of Record's name and align it with the applicable MOHRE or free-zone contract and employment framework.
4. Work Permit, Visa and RegistrationObtain the work permit and support medical, Emirates ID and residence visa steps for expatriate employees; register eligible UAE nationals with the applicable pension authority.
5. Monthly Payroll and WPS ExecutionCalculate contractual pay, process WPS salary payments where applicable, administer pension contributions for covered UAE nationals and maintain payroll records.
6. Ongoing Compliance AdministrationAdminister leave, benefits, visa renewals, medical insurance, WPS, Emiratisation obligations and employment updates as the relationship evolves.
7. Offboarding or TransitionProcess termination in line with UAE labour law and contract terms, calculate end-of-service benefits or pension implications, cancel work permits and residence visas where applicable and support transfer to the client's own UAE entity where one is later established.
Typical OutputsEmployment contracts, work permits, residence visas, Emirates ID records, WPS payroll records, GPSSA or relevant pension records, end-of-service benefit calculations and termination documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine whether an Employer of Record is the correct route in the United Arab Emirates. It is presented as a logical workflow so that the reader can follow the sequence as an operational progression rather than as disconnected legal labels.

  1. Identify whether the business needs an employment relationship or an independent contractor engagement in the United Arab Emirates.
  2. Confirm whether the business already has, or intends to establish, its own mainland UAE entity or free-zone entity.
  3. If no local entity exists or is planned in the short term, assess whether an Employer of Record can lawfully support the intended role under the relevant UAE framework.
  4. Confirm whether the employee will work in the mainland UAE or within a specified free zone and identify the applicable employment and immigration authority.
  5. Confirm whether the worker is a UAE national, GCC national or expatriate, and determine the resulting pension, end-of-service benefit, work permit and residence visa implications.
  6. Set up contract, visa, WPS, pension and payroll processes, then align ongoing administration with actual working arrangements.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance explains why Employer of Record work in the United Arab Emirates cannot be understood only as a domestic payroll matter. The UAE has a large expatriate workforce and functions as a regional hub, so employee nationality, immigration sponsorship, tax residence, free-zone status, GCC pension coordination and regional mobility often require cross-jurisdiction analysis from the outset.

RecognitionUAE Employer of Record arrangements often function as one layer within a broader Gulf, Middle East or international hiring strategy rather than an isolated domestic payroll exercise.
Expatriate WorkforceMost private-sector employees are expatriates whose lawful employment depends on the sponsoring employer's work permit and residence visa process, as well as timely permit maintenance and cancellation.
Mainland and Free-Zone DistinctionThe applicable authority, employment contract, payroll process, visa sponsorship and employment rules may differ depending on whether the legal employer is mainland-based or located in a particular free zone.
International RulesTax residence, double taxation arrangements, GCC pension coordination, foreign employee home-country social security and regional mobility frequently shape Employer of Record planning where the client or worker has connections beyond the UAE.
Practical ConsiderationsCross-border Employer of Record arrangements usually work best when UAE employment, payroll, WPS, visa sponsorship, pension or end-of-service benefit treatment and the client's home-country obligations are treated as one coordinated compliance architecture.
Typical RisksAssuming that a single UAE payroll configuration or employment contract automatically resolves mainland versus free-zone, visa, WPS, pension, tax residence and regional mobility questions.
Key Takeaways
  • The United Arab Emirates often functions as one hiring location within a wider Gulf, Middle East or international Employer of Record strategy rather than a standalone engagement.
  • Employee nationality, mainland or free-zone structure, work permit and residence visa status, WPS and pension or end-of-service treatment may all be relevant within the same employment relationship.
  • Payroll, work authorisation and cross-border workforce planning need to be aligned, not handled as separate issues.
Operating Constraints & Risks

Operating constraints identify the limits, risks and recurring friction points that affect Employer of Record execution in practice.

Classification RiskTreating a worker as an Employer of Record employee while the underlying relationship is structured or supervised like an independent contractor can create labour, immigration and commercial exposure.
Jurisdiction Selection RiskUsing a mainland or free-zone employment structure that does not match the actual work location, business activity or client operating model can create regulatory and visa exposure.
WPS RiskFailing to pay agreed wages correctly and on time through WPS where applicable can create MOHRE enforcement, work permit and labour compliance consequences.
Pension and End-of-Service RiskFailing to distinguish UAE national pension obligations from expatriate end-of-service benefit obligations can create underpayment or statutory benefits exposure.
Visa RiskEmploying an expatriate without a valid work permit and residence visa, or failing to cancel or renew immigration documentation correctly, can create serious employer compliance exposure.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does the United Arab Emirates Have a Universal Statutory Minimum Wage That an Employer of Record Must Apply?No single universal statutory minimum wage figure applies across the UAE private sector. Salary must be stated in the employment contract and paid through the Wage Protection System where applicable. Emiratisation and work permit salary conditions may create separate requirements for UAE national employees and specific employment categories.
What Pension Contributions Must an Employer of Record Make in the United Arab Emirates?For eligible UAE national employees covered by the post-2023 GPSSA regime, the employer contribution is generally 15 percent and the employee contribution is 11 percent of contribution-account salary. Earlier and emirate-specific pension regimes may differ. Expatriate employees are generally not covered by GPSSA and instead receive end-of-service benefits or an applicable alternative savings scheme.
What Is the Wage Protection System in the UAE?The Wage Protection System is an electronic salary transfer mechanism through which private-sector employers in the mainland UAE generally pay wages through approved financial institutions. It is administered in connection with Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation wage compliance requirements.
Can a Foreign Company Use an Employer of Record Instead of Establishing a UAE Entity?Yes. A foreign company may use an Employer of Record to employ staff in the UAE without first establishing its own local entity, subject to the specific mainland or free-zone employment framework, payroll, visa, WPS and labour law requirements.
Is Payroll Alone Enough for UAE Employer of Record Compliance?No. Correct Employer of Record compliance also requires the right mainland or free-zone structure, valid work permits and residence visas for expatriates, WPS compliance where applicable, correct pension or end-of-service benefit treatment and compliant employment contracts.
