Executive Summary

An Employer of Record in Slovenia is a structured arrangement in which a local entity becomes the formal legal employer of a worker who performs services for a client business, while the client retains day-to-day direction of the work itself. The function exists because engaging staff directly in Slovenia normally requires a registered employer, a payroll infrastructure and ongoing compliance with Slovenian employment, tax and social insurance rules.

Operationally, the Employer of Record issues the Slovenian employment contract, registers the employee for mandatory social insurance through the relevant systems, calculates payroll, administers income-tax withholding and social contributions through FURS, and manages statutory employment obligations such as annual leave and the mandatory annual holiday allowance known as regres.

The Slovenian legal framework for this function is anchored in the Employment Relationships Act (ZDR-1), the Minimum Wage Act (ZMinP), the mandatory social insurance framework and payroll rules administered by the Financial Administration of the Republic of Slovenia. For 2026, the statutory gross minimum wage is EUR 1,481.88 per month.

Cross-border relevance is substantial because many Employer of Record clients are foreign companies without a Slovenian legal entity. These businesses rely on the Employer of Record to lawfully employ staff in Slovenia, coordinate EU social security rules such as A1 certificates and, where relevant, support the single residence and work permit process for non-EU nationals.

Purpose

The purpose of the Employer of Record function is to allow a business to lawfully engage workers in Slovenia without first establishing its own Slovenian legal entity, while ensuring that payroll, tax withholding, social contributions, regres and employment law obligations are met correctly from the outset.

It exists to convert a hiring intention into a compliant Slovenian employment relationship, reducing the administrative burden and misclassification risk that would otherwise fall on a foreign business unfamiliar with Slovenian payroll and labour law.

Primary Outcome

A compliant Slovenian employment relationship in which the worker holds a valid local employment contract, payroll and statutory contributions are administered correctly, minimum wage and mandatory holiday allowance obligations are respected, and the client business retains operational direction of the work without carrying local employer-of-record liability.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Employer of Record work usually progresses from onboarding intent to ongoing payroll administration and eventual offboarding. It matters because Employer of Record work is a continuous operating relationship, not a single filing event.

1. Client and Role AssessmentConfirm the client's hiring intent, the role, reporting line and whether the arrangement will genuinely function as an employment relationship in Slovenia.
2. Minimum Wage and Regres MappingConfirm the applicable minimum wage, mandatory annual holiday allowance and proposed salary, working time and benefit terms.
3. Contract IssuanceIssue a Slovenian employment contract in the Employer of Record's name, specifying role, salary, working time, leave and notice terms in accordance with the Employment Relationships Act.
4. Tax and Insurance RegistrationConfirm FURS registration, register the employee for mandatory social insurance and complete applicable onboarding administration before work begins.
5. Monthly Payroll ExecutionCalculate gross pay, withhold income tax, calculate employer and employee social contributions, make required payroll submissions and remit liabilities.
6. Ongoing Compliance AdministrationAdminister leave, regres, sick leave coordination, benefits, working-time records and employment updates as the relationship evolves.
7. Offboarding or TransitionProcess termination in line with Slovenian employment law, complete relevant deregistration and support transfer of the employee to the client's own Slovenian entity where one is later established.
Typical OutputsSigned employment contracts, social insurance registration records, monthly payslips, FURS payroll filings, statutory contribution remittances, leave and regres records and termination documentation.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Slovenia Have a Statutory Minimum Wage That an Employer of Record Must Apply?Yes. Slovenia has a statutory gross monthly minimum wage of EUR 1,481.88 for 2026. The Minimum Wage Act applies to workers employed in Slovenia, with specific statutory exclusions for some payment elements.
Who Administers Payroll Tax and Social Contributions for an Employer of Record in Slovenia?The Financial Administration of the Republic of Slovenia (FURS) administers tax and contribution filings. Mandatory social insurance enrolment is coordinated through the Health Insurance Institute of Slovenia (ZZZS), while the employer makes the applicable payroll submissions and payments.
What Is Mandatory Regres in Slovenia?Regres is a mandatory annual holiday allowance for employees entitled to annual leave. It must generally be paid at least in the amount of the statutory minimum wage, subject to applicable legal timing rules and any more favourable collective agreement.
Can a Foreign Company Use an Employer of Record Instead of Establishing a Slovenian Entity?Yes. A foreign company may use an Employer of Record to employ staff in Slovenia without first incorporating a Slovenian entity, subject to Slovenian employment, payroll, tax, social insurance and work-authorisation requirements.
Is Payroll Filing Alone Enough for Compliance?No. Correct Employer of Record compliance also requires valid employment contracts, mandatory social insurance registration, minimum wage and regres compliance and, where relevant, cross-border social security coordination.
