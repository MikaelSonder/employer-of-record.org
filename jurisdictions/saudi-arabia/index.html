Executive Summary

An Employer of Record in Saudi Arabia is a structured arrangement in which a local entity becomes the formal legal employer of a worker who performs services for a client business, while the client retains day-to-day direction of the work itself. The function exists because employing staff in Saudi Arabia normally requires a compliant local employment relationship, payroll infrastructure, GOSI registration, Wage Protection System administration and, for expatriates, work permit and iqama sponsorship.

Operationally, the Employer of Record issues the Saudi employment contract, maintains employee data and contract records through Qiwa, administers payroll through the Wage Protection System and Mudad where applicable, calculates GOSI contributions, manages Saudisation obligations and sponsors work permits, visas and iqama procedures for expatriate employees.

The Saudi legal framework for this function is anchored in the Saudi Labour Law, Social Insurance Law, employment and residence regulations and Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development rules. Saudi Arabia does not operate a universal private-sector minimum wage applying equally to all employees; contractual pay, Saudisation and programme-specific salary rules must be considered instead.

Cross-border relevance is substantial because Saudi Arabia has a large expatriate workforce and is a major regional business market. Employer of Record arrangements may require coordination of Saudi payroll, GOSI, work permit and iqama requirements, Saudi national employment quotas and regional mobility across the Gulf.

Scope

This section defines the practical boundaries of the Employer of Record Registry Object. The purpose is to distinguish Employer of Record work as an operational employment, payroll and immigration discipline from broader corporate advisory work, recruitment agency placement or general HR consulting.

Covered MattersSaudi employment contract issuance, Qiwa and Mudad administration, GOSI registration and contributions, WPS payroll, Saudisation compliance, work permit and iqama sponsorship, leave, termination processing and end-of-service benefit administration.
Functional BoundaryThe Registry Object covers how a Saudi Employer of Record legally employs and administers workers on behalf of a client business without the client establishing its own Saudi legal entity.
Related but Not PrimaryRecruitment and candidate sourcing, outsourcing and manpower supply activities, general management consulting, tax structuring unrelated to payroll and commercial contract drafting between the client and its own customers may connect to the topic but are not treated here as the primary object.
Outside ScopeIndependent contractor engagement without an employment relationship, generic HR software implementation and business activities unrelated to formal legal employment in Saudi Arabia.
Purpose

The purpose of the Employer of Record function is to allow a business to lawfully engage workers in Saudi Arabia without first establishing its own Saudi legal entity, while ensuring that employment contracts, payroll, GOSI, WPS, work permits, iqama and labour law obligations are met correctly from the outset.

It exists to convert a hiring intention into a compliant Saudi employment relationship, reducing the administrative burden and immigration and misclassification risk that would otherwise fall on a foreign business unfamiliar with Saudi payroll, employment and visa law.

Primary Outcome

A compliant Saudi employment relationship in which the worker holds a valid local employment contract and, where required, work permit and iqama; payroll and WPS obligations are administered correctly; GOSI, Saudisation and end-of-service benefit obligations are respected; and the client business retains operational direction of the work without carrying local employer-of-record liability.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how Employer of Record services operate in Saudi Arabia. The section matters because Saudi employment practice combines a predominantly expatriate workforce, employer-sponsored immigration, WPS salary payment monitoring, GOSI nationality-based treatment and Saudisation workforce requirements.

Operational CultureSaudi employment practice is formal, sponsor-led and digitally administered through platforms including Qiwa, Mudad and Muqeem. Employment contracts, work permits, iqama, WPS payroll and GOSI are central to compliant employer operations.
Legal Framework OrientationThe Saudi Labour Law and Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development regulations establish core private-sector employment obligations. Saudi national workforce participation is also shaped by Nitaqat and Saudisation requirements.
Commercial ContextPayroll cost planning differs by employee nationality and GOSI registration regime. Saudi nationals require GOSI and may affect Saudisation calculations, while expatriate employees require work permit, iqama, medical insurance and end-of-service benefit planning.
Language ExpectationArabic is the official language and critical for statutory administration and local employment documentation. English is widely used in international business and may be used alongside Arabic in employment documents.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Employer of Record work usually progresses from onboarding intent to ongoing payroll administration and eventual offboarding. It matters because Employer of Record work is a continuous employment, payroll and immigration relationship, not a single filing event.

1. Client, Role and Employee Category AssessmentConfirm the client's hiring intent, role, reporting line, employee nationality and whether the arrangement will genuinely function as an employment relationship in Saudi Arabia.
2. Saudisation, GOSI and Visa MappingConfirm Nitaqat and Saudisation implications, applicable GOSI regime, contributory wage, contractual terms, work permit category, iqama route and WPS requirements.
3. Contract IssuanceIssue the Saudi employment contract in the Employer of Record's name and register or manage the contract through Qiwa in accordance with applicable requirements.
4. Work Permit, Visa, Iqama and GOSI RegistrationFor expatriates, obtain the work permit, visa, medical insurance and iqama; for covered employees, complete GOSI registration and any applicable Saudisation data updates.
5. Monthly Payroll and WPS ExecutionCalculate contractual pay, process WPS salary payments, calculate GOSI contributions by employee category and maintain payroll and wage records.
6. Ongoing Compliance AdministrationAdminister leave, benefits, WPS, GOSI, work permit and iqama renewals, Saudisation requirements and employment updates as the relationship evolves.
7. Offboarding or TransitionProcess termination in line with Saudi Labour Law and contract terms, calculate end-of-service benefits, complete GOSI and Qiwa updates, cancel work permits and iqama where applicable and support transfer to the client's own Saudi entity where one is later established.
Typical OutputsEmployment contracts, Qiwa records, work permits, visas, iqama, GOSI registration records, WPS payroll records, end-of-service benefit calculations and termination documentation.

  1. Identify whether the business needs an employment relationship or an independent contractor engagement in Saudi Arabia.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance explains why Employer of Record work in Saudi Arabia cannot be understood only as a domestic payroll matter. Saudi Arabia has a large expatriate workforce and functions as a major Gulf market, so employee nationality, immigration sponsorship, GOSI, Saudisation, tax residence, GCC pension coordination and regional mobility often require cross-jurisdiction analysis from the outset.

RecognitionSaudi Employer of Record arrangements often function as one layer within a broader Gulf, Middle East or international hiring strategy rather than an isolated domestic payroll exercise.
Expatriate WorkforceExpatriate employees require valid work permits, visas and iqama sponsored by the legal employer, with the employer bearing material responsibilities for immigration administration and renewals.
Nationality-Based TreatmentEmployee nationality affects GOSI contribution treatment, Saudisation classification, potential GCC pension coordination and end-of-service benefit planning.
International RulesTax residence, GCC pension coordination, foreign employee home-country social security, visa sponsorship and regional mobility frequently shape Employer of Record planning where the client or worker has connections beyond Saudi Arabia.
Practical ConsiderationsCross-border Employer of Record arrangements usually work best when Saudi employment, payroll, WPS, GOSI, visa sponsorship, Saudisation and the client's home-country obligations are treated as one coordinated compliance architecture.
Typical RisksAssuming that a single Saudi payroll configuration or employment contract automatically resolves GOSI regime, nationality, visa, iqama, Saudisation, tax residence and regional mobility questions.
Key Takeaways
  • Saudi Arabia often functions as one hiring location within a wider Gulf, Middle East or international Employer of Record strategy rather than a standalone engagement.
  • Employee nationality, GOSI regime, Saudisation status, work permit and iqama, WPS and end-of-service benefit treatment may all be relevant within the same employment relationship.
  • Payroll, work authorisation and cross-border workforce planning need to be aligned, not handled as separate issues.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Saudi Arabia Have a Universal Private-Sector Minimum Wage That an Employer of Record Must Apply?Saudi Arabia does not operate a universal private-sector minimum wage applying equally to every employee. Salary terms are set contractually, while Saudisation, Nitaqat and programme-specific rules may impose salary requirements for Saudi nationals and specified employment categories.
What GOSI Contributions Must an Employer of Record Make in Saudi Arabia?GOSI treatment depends on employee nationality and registration regime. For Saudi nationals under the new social insurance regime, from 1 July 2026 the employer contribution is 12.75 percent and employee contribution is 10.75 percent of contributory wage. Saudi nationals in the older regime generally remain at 11.75 percent employer and 9.75 percent employee. For non-Saudi employees, the employer normally pays 2 percent Occupational Hazards insurance.
What Is the Wage Protection System in Saudi Arabia?Saudi Arabia's Wage Protection System is an electronic system that monitors private-sector salary payments. Employers process payroll through approved banks or financial institutions and submit wage information in accordance with Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development requirements.
Can a Foreign Company Use an Employer of Record Instead of Establishing a Saudi Entity?Yes. A foreign company may use an Employer of Record to employ staff in Saudi Arabia without first establishing its own Saudi entity, subject to Saudi employment, GOSI, WPS, Saudisation, work permit, iqama and labour law requirements.
Is Payroll Alone Enough for Saudi Employer of Record Compliance?No. Correct Employer of Record compliance also requires the correct GOSI employee classification, Qiwa administration, WPS where applicable, Saudisation assessment, valid work permits and iqama for expatriates, compliant employment contracts and correct end-of-service benefit treatment.
