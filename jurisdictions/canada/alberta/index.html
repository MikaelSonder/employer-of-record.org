Executive Summary

An Employer of Record in Alberta is a structured arrangement in which a Canadian entity becomes the formal legal employer of a worker who performs services for a client business, while the client retains day-to-day direction of the work itself. The function exists because engaging staff directly in Alberta normally requires a registered employer, Canadian payroll infrastructure and ongoing compliance with federal and Alberta employment, insurance and workplace rules.

Operationally, the Employer of Record issues the Alberta employment agreement, establishes a Canada Revenue Agency payroll program account, calculates and withholds income tax, Canada Pension Plan and Employment Insurance amounts, remits employer contributions, maintains WCB Alberta coverage where required and prepares records of employment at the end of insurable employment.

The Alberta legal framework for this function is anchored in the Employment Standards Code, the Employment Standards Regulation, the Occupational Health and Safety Act, the Workers' Compensation Act and the Alberta Human Rights Act, together with the federal Income Tax Act, Canada Pension Plan and Employment Insurance Act. Alberta's general minimum wage is CAD 15.00 per hour. Alberta does not impose a provincial payroll tax or employer health tax.

Cross-border relevance is substantial because many Employer of Record clients are foreign companies without a Canadian legal entity. These businesses rely on the Employer of Record to lawfully employ staff in Alberta, coordinate Canadian payroll deductions, work permits and social-security considerations, and administer Alberta-specific employment standards and workers' compensation requirements.

Purpose

The purpose of the Employer of Record function is to allow a business to lawfully engage workers in Alberta without first establishing its own Canadian legal entity, while ensuring that payroll, income-tax withholding, CPP, EI, WCB Alberta and employment standards obligations are met correctly from the outset.

It exists to convert a hiring intention into a compliant Alberta employment relationship, reducing the administrative burden and misclassification risk that would otherwise fall on a foreign business unfamiliar with Canadian payroll and Alberta employment law.

Primary Outcome

A compliant Alberta employment relationship in which the worker holds a valid local employment agreement, payroll and statutory deductions are administered correctly, minimum wage, Employment Standards Code and workers' compensation obligations are respected, and the client business retains operational direction of the work without carrying local employer-of-record liability.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Employer of Record work usually progresses from onboarding intent to ongoing payroll administration and eventual offboarding. It matters because Employer of Record work is a continuous operating relationship, not a single filing event.

1. Client and Role AssessmentConfirm the client's hiring intent, the role, reporting line and whether the arrangement will genuinely function as an employment relationship in Alberta.
2. Minimum Wage and Terms MappingConfirm the applicable Alberta minimum wage category, hours, overtime, vacation, statutory leave, work authorisation and proposed employment terms.
3. Agreement IssuanceIssue an Alberta employment agreement in the Employer of Record's name, specifying role, compensation, working time, vacation, notice terms and core employment standards terms.
4. CRA and Alberta Account SetupEstablish the CRA payroll program account, collect federal and Alberta TD1 information, assess WCB Alberta coverage and complete relevant work-authorisation processes.
5. Payroll ExecutionCalculate gross pay, deduct income tax, CPP and EI, calculate employer CPP and EI contributions, apply required payroll obligations and pay net wages.
6. Ongoing Compliance AdministrationAdminister vacation pay, general holidays, statutory leaves, WCB Alberta reporting and premium payments, records of employment and payroll recordkeeping.
7. Offboarding or TransitionProcess termination in line with the Employment Standards Code, including written notice or termination pay, final payroll and record of employment administration, or support transfer to the client's own Canadian entity where one is later established.
Typical OutputsSigned employment agreements, CRA payroll account records, TD1 forms, payslips, CPP and EI remittances, WCB Alberta records, records of employment, T4s and termination documentation.

Operating Constraints & Risks

Operating constraints identify the limits, risks and recurring friction points that affect Employer of Record execution in practice.

Classification RiskTreating a worker as an independent contractor where the actual relationship has the characteristics of employment can create tax, CPP, EI and employment standards exposure.
CRA Payroll RiskFailing to establish a CRA payroll program account or to remit income tax, CPP and EI deductions on time can create penalties, interest and payroll compliance exposure.
Minimum Wage Category RiskApplying the incorrect minimum wage by failing to identify a qualifying student, salesperson or domestic employee category can create underpayment exposure.

Final Pay RiskFailing to pay final earnings within the chosen statutory payment period can create Employment Standards Code exposure. Final earnings include unpaid wages, overtime, vacation pay, general holiday pay and termination pay where owed.
Cross-Border RiskOverlooking work permit, Canadian tax residence, CPP social-security or cross-border United States payroll questions can create immigration, payroll and administrative exposure for mobile workers.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Alberta Have a Statutory Minimum Wage That an Employer of Record Must Apply?Yes. Alberta's general minimum wage is CAD 15.00 per hour for most employees. A reduced rate of CAD 13.00 per hour applies to qualifying students under 18, subject to restrictions. Separate weekly and monthly minimum rates apply to certain salespersons and live-in domestic employees.
Who Administers Payroll Deductions and Statutory Contributions for an Employer of Record in Alberta?The Canada Revenue Agency administers income tax, Canada Pension Plan and Employment Insurance deductions and remittances. Alberta does not levy a provincial payroll tax or employer health tax. WCB Alberta administers workers' compensation for covered employers.
Is Alberta Employer Registration Mandatory for an Employer of Record Operating in Alberta?Yes. An employer must establish a Canada Revenue Agency payroll program account before making payroll deductions. WCB Alberta registration is required for employers in mandatory coverage industries. Alberta has no provincial payroll tax or employer health tax registration.
When Are Final Earnings Due in Alberta?When employment ends, Alberta employers may choose to pay final earnings within 10 calendar days after the end of the pay period in which termination occurred, or within 31 calendar days after the employee's last day of employment.
Is Payroll Remittance Alone Enough for Compliance?No. Correct Employer of Record compliance also requires compliant employment terms, Alberta minimum wage alignment, Employment Standards Code administration, WCB Alberta assessment, work authorisation checks and, where relevant, cross-border tax and social-security coordination.
