Executive Summary

An Employer of Record in Bulgaria is a structured arrangement in which a local entity becomes the formal legal employer of a worker who performs services for a client business, while the client retains day-to-day direction of the work itself. The function exists because engaging staff directly in Bulgaria normally requires a registered employer, a payroll infrastructure and ongoing compliance with Bulgarian employment, tax, social security and health insurance rules.

Operationally, the Employer of Record issues the Bulgarian employment contract, registers the employee for compulsory social and health insurance, calculates payroll, withholds income tax, calculates statutory contributions and submits the relevant payroll declarations to the National Revenue Agency.

The Bulgarian legal framework for this function is anchored in the Labour Code, the Social Insurance Code, the Health Insurance Act and the statutory minimum wage framework. From 1 January 2026, the national gross minimum wage is BGN 1,213 per month and the minimum hourly wage is BGN 7.31 for normal working time.

Cross-border relevance is substantial because many Employer of Record clients are foreign companies without a Bulgarian legal entity. These businesses rely on the Employer of Record to lawfully employ staff in Bulgaria, coordinate EU social security rules such as A1 certificates and, where relevant, support the single residence and work permit process for third-country nationals.

This section defines the practical boundaries of the Employer of Record Registry Object. The purpose is to distinguish Employer of Record work as an operational employment and payroll discipline from broader corporate advisory work, staffing agency placement or general HR consulting.

Purpose

The purpose of the Employer of Record function is to allow a business to lawfully engage workers in Bulgaria without first establishing its own Bulgarian legal entity, while ensuring that payroll, tax withholding, social and health insurance contributions and employment law obligations are met correctly from the outset.

It exists to convert a hiring intention into a compliant Bulgarian employment relationship, reducing the administrative burden and misclassification risk that would otherwise fall on a foreign business unfamiliar with Bulgarian payroll and labour law.

Primary Outcome

A compliant Bulgarian employment relationship in which the worker holds a valid local employment contract, payroll and statutory contributions are administered correctly, minimum wage and Labour Code obligations are respected, and the client business retains operational direction of the work without carrying local employer-of-record liability.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics explain the jurisdiction-specific features that shape how Employer of Record services operate in Bulgaria. The section matters because Bulgarian employment practice is influenced by a detailed Labour Code, mandatory social and health insurance, a national minimum wage and centralised payroll contribution administration.

Operational CultureBulgarian employment practice is statute-driven and document-oriented, with employer obligations administered through the National Revenue Agency, social insurance institutions and labour inspection systems.
Legal Framework OrientationStatutory employment protection under the Labour Code operates alongside a national minimum wage and collective agreements that may establish more favourable sectoral conditions.
Commercial ContextPayroll cost planning requires attention to the flat personal income tax rate, sector-dependent employer social and health contribution rates and the statutory maximum insurance income threshold.
Language ExpectationBulgarian is required for statutory administration and is ordinarily used in local employment documentation, while English is frequently used in international business, group HR policy and cross-border payroll coordination.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Employer of Record work usually progresses from onboarding intent to ongoing payroll administration and eventual offboarding. It matters because Employer of Record work is a continuous operating relationship, not a single filing event.

1. Client and Role AssessmentConfirm the client's hiring intent, the role, reporting line and whether the arrangement will genuinely function as an employment relationship in Bulgaria.
2. Minimum Wage and Terms MappingConfirm the applicable statutory minimum wage, any relevant collective agreement and proposed salary, working time and benefit terms.
3. Contract IssuanceIssue a Bulgarian employment contract in the Employer of Record's name, specifying role, salary, working time, leave and notice terms in accordance with the Labour Code.
4. Tax and Insurance RegistrationConfirm National Revenue Agency registration, register the employee for compulsory social and health insurance and complete applicable onboarding administration before work begins.
5. Monthly Payroll ExecutionCalculate gross pay, withhold income tax, calculate employer and employee social and health contributions, make required payroll declarations and remit liabilities.
6. Ongoing Compliance AdministrationAdminister leave, sick leave coordination, benefits, working-time records and employment updates as the relationship evolves.
7. Offboarding or TransitionProcess termination in line with Bulgarian employment law, complete relevant deregistration and support transfer of the employee to the client's own Bulgarian entity where one is later established.
Typical OutputsSigned employment contracts, employee registration records, monthly payslips, payroll declarations, statutory contribution remittances, leave records and termination documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine whether an Employer of Record is the correct route in Bulgaria. It is presented as a logical workflow so that the reader can follow the sequence as an operational progression rather than as disconnected legal labels.

  1. Identify whether the business needs an employment relationship or an independent contractor engagement in Bulgaria.
  2. Confirm whether the business already has, or intends to establish, its own Bulgarian legal entity.
  3. If no local entity exists or is planned in the short term, assess whether an Employer of Record can lawfully support the intended role.
  4. Confirm the applicable statutory minimum wage and whether a collective agreement sets more favourable terms.
  5. Confirm whether the worker is an EU/EEA national or requires a Bulgarian single residence and work permit.
  6. Set up National Revenue Agency, social insurance, health insurance and payroll processes, then align ongoing administration with actual working arrangements.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Bulgaria Have a Statutory Minimum Wage That an Employer of Record Must Apply?Yes. Bulgaria's national minimum wage is BGN 1,213 gross per month and BGN 7.31 per hour from 1 January 2026. Every employer must meet or exceed the applicable statutory minimum.
Who Administers Payroll Tax and Social Contributions for an Employer of Record in Bulgaria?The National Revenue Agency administers payroll tax and compulsory social and health insurance contribution collection. The National Social Security Institute and National Health Insurance Fund administer the corresponding social insurance and health insurance systems.
What Are the Principal Employer Contribution Rates in Bulgaria?Employer social security and health insurance contributions generally range from 18.92 percent to 19.62 percent of gross insurable income, depending mainly on the occupational accident and disease rate applicable to the employer's sector.
Can a Foreign Company Use an Employer of Record Instead of Establishing a Bulgarian Entity?Yes. A foreign company may use an Employer of Record to employ staff in Bulgaria without first incorporating a Bulgarian entity, subject to Bulgarian employment, payroll, tax, insurance and work-authorisation requirements.
Is Payroll Filing Alone Enough for Compliance?No. Correct Employer of Record compliance also requires valid employment contracts, compulsory social and health insurance registration, minimum wage alignment, Labour Code compliance and, where relevant, cross-border social security coordination.
